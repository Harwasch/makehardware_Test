1.  SERIES COMPENSATION -- what the boards actually fit
==============================================================================
  TX  PORT_OUT+ :  300.0 nF  (3 parts in parallel)
        C9    100nF      B32921C3104M000
        C25   100nF      B32921C3104M000
        C26   100nF      B32921C3104M000
  RX  PORT+     :  300.0 nF  (3 parts in parallel)
        C1    100nF      B32921C3104M000
        C2    100nF      B32921C3104M000
        C3    100nF      B32921C3104M000

  Series-series compensation resonates at f = 1/(2*pi*sqrt(L*C)).
  Solving for the coil inductance each capacitor bank implies:

      f (kHz)   L_tx (uH)   L_rx (uH)
           20      211.09      211.09
           40       52.77       52.77
           85       11.69       11.69
          100        8.44        8.44
          150        3.75        3.75

  The repo's coil (ADR-0001) is 172 uH per side.
  With 300 nF that resonates at 22.2 kHz,
  not the 85 kHz the requirements assume.
  85 kHz with a 172 uH coil needs 20.4 nF, i.e. 15x less than fitted.

3.  DAB -- the HV bridge output loop as drawn
==============================================================================
  L1 = 47 uH  (IHLP6767GZER470M11, IND-SMD_L17.2-W17.2)
  Net trace from the HV bridge:
      AH.S/AL.D  -> D1_1 -> L1.1
      L1.2       -> S$194154 -> U94.IN+   (TMCS1133 current sensor)
      U94.IN-    -> BH_3 -> BH.S/BL.D
  That is the whole loop.  There is no transformer, and no pad or
  connector, between the two bridge legs -- the sheet closes the
  bridge output on itself through L1 and the sense resistor path.

  Driven as a bridge at 85 kHz from 400 V with no
  load impedance but L1, the current ramps
      di/dt        = V/L = 8.51 A/us
      peak-to-peak = V/(2*f*L) = 50 A
  L1 is an IHLP-6767 class part in a 17.2 x 17.2 mm footprint;
  50 A pk-pk is far outside anything that package saturates at.

==============================================================================
4.  DAB -- LV bridge switch nodes
==============================================================================
  Net SEC carries: AGD3, AGD4, A_H_PD3, A_H_PD4, CBA3, CBA4, CH1, CH2, CL1, CL2, D11, D3, DH1, DH2, DL1, DL2

  CH1/CH2 sources, DH1/DH2 sources, CL1/CL2 drains and DL1/DL2 drains
  are all one node.  Leg C and leg D therefore share a switch node,
  while AGD3 (leg D) and AGD4 (leg C) are driven from separate
  MCU outputs C/C# and D/D#.
  The same 24 pads carry net SEC on the PCB, so this is the layout
  too, not a schematic-only slip.

  Consequence: any phase difference between the C and D gate pairs
  turns on a high-side FET of one leg and a low-side FET of the
  other across LV_BUS with nothing between them.

6.  CONNECTOR CURRENT RATING vs THE CURRENT THE NET CARRIES
==============================================================================
  Amass XT-series connectors are named for their CURRENT rating,
  not their voltage: TME/Amass list XT60 at 30 A continuous / 500 V DC
  and XT30 at 15 A continuous / 500 V DC.  Voltage is fine on a 400 V
  bus; current is where these land.

  DAB U82  XT60-M.        on HV_BUS            3.0 kW at   400 V =    7.5 A vs   30 A
  DAB U83  XT60-M.        on LV_BUS            3.0 kW at    48 V =   62.5 A vs   30 A  <-- OVER
  DAB CN1  XT30UW-F.G.Y   on 48V aux only      0.1 kW at    48 V =    1.0 A vs   15 A
  RX  CN3  XT60PB-F.G.Y   on VCC (link out)    3.0 kW at   400 V =    7.5 A vs   30 A

  CN1 only feeds the five TPSM560R6 aux converters, so its 15 A is
  ample.  The DAB's LV output connector is the binding one: 62.5 A
  through a 30 A part.

7.  TX POWER STAGE HEADROOM -- LMG2640, SNOSDH5 (Nov 2024)
==============================================================================
  Recommended operating condition ID(cnts) = +/-8.2 A, both FETs.
  Cycle-by-cycle over-current threshold IT(OC) = 8.2 A min,
  9.1 A typ, 10 A max -- the part shuts its own gate off above this.
  RDS(on) = 105 mohm at 25 C, 200 mohm at 125 C.  RthJA = 22.8 C/W.

  A 400 V bridge's fundamental is 360 V rms.  At unity power
  factor the deliverable power is V1 * I1_rms, and the peak of a
  sinusoidal tank current is sqrt(2) x its rms:

     OCP trip (A pk)  I1 rms (A)    P (W)  P_FET at 125C (W)
                 8.2        5.80     2088                3.4
                 9.1        6.43     2317                4.1
                10.0        7.07     2546                5.0

  3 kW needs 8.3 A rms = 11.8 A peak, which is above even the
  10 A best-case trip point.  The TX cannot reach 3 kW from a 400 V
  bus with one LMG2640 per leg, whatever the tank is tuned to.

  Conduction loss alone at 3 kW is 6.9 W per FET, 13.9 W per package.
  On the datasheet's RthJA of 22.8 C/W that is a 319 C rise; the part
  has dual thermal pads and a real board does far better than the
  JEDEC number, but even a good 8-10 C/W path puts the junction over
  150 C before any switching loss is counted.  Thermals need a
  measurement, not an estimate -- and that is a lab task.

8.  DAB LV BRIDGE CONDUCTION LOSS AT 3 kW  (ELE-003)
==============================================================================
  The board fits 8 x BSC190N15NS3G-TP: CH1, CH2, CL1, CL2, DH1, DH2, DL1, DL2
  Two per switch position, four positions -- N = 2 per branch.
  Second-sourced from TECH PUBLIC(台舟) under the Infineon part
  number, which needs its own qualification.

  3 kW into 48 V is 62.5 A dc.
  Best-case tank rms = (pi/2sqrt2) x I_dc = 69.4 A.
  R_ds(on) 19 mohm max at 25 C, x1.6 at 100 C = 30.4 mohm.

  Each switch position conducts for half the cycle, so the whole
  four-position bridge costs 2 * I_rms^2 * R_ds / N:

     N per branch  devices   conduction (W)   vs 100 W budget
                2        8              147              1.5x
                4       16               73              0.7x
                6       24               49              0.5x

  The board's two per branch spends 1.5x the entire ELE-003 budget
  on low-voltage conduction alone, before the HV bridge, the
  transformer, or any switching loss.  Four per branch is under the
  budget but leaves almost nothing for the rest.
