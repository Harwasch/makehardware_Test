ULYSSES LARS · REV B v1 design review & v2 architecture · 28 Aug 2026

3 kW is reachable.
Not with the v1 magnetics.

A review of the Mako 2.0 wireless charger against its primary sources — the white paper, the two firmware repositories, and the vendor datasheets — followed by the vision, plan, requirements and architecture for a second revision.

Three findings block the power target. All three are the same category of error, and none of them is a tuning problem.

In each case a component was carried over from an application it suits into one it does not: a GaN half bridge specified for 75 W flyback converters driving a 3 kW resonant bridge, and an induction-hob coil geometry used as a 400 V power link. The white paper reports bench results for resonance tracking only, and no power, efficiency, coupling or thermal measurement appears anywhere in it — so 3 kW should be treated as an unproven target rather than a demonstrated capability.

What the review found

three blocking · eight further
F8blocking

The coil is 13× too small, and it fights the control loop

For a series–series compensated link with both sides tuned, the power delivered at resonance is P = V₁·V₂ / (ω·M). At a 400 V bus the fundamental is 360 V RMS on each end, and the v1 coil pair at k = 0.5 gives M = 6 µH, so ωM = 3.2 Ω and

P = 360 × 360 / 3.2 = 40.5 kW, at a tank current of 112 A RMS

That is not a link capable of 40 kW. It means the pair is grossly under-inductive, and holding it to 3 kW requires running far away from resonance — the exact opposite of what the v1 firmware exists to do. The magnetics and the resonance tracker are working against each other.

The cause is named in the white paper itself: p.6 records that the coil geometry came from a paper on an induction stove. A hob coil is deliberately low-inductance and high-current, tightly coupled to a steel pan. Correct for that job; wrong as the starting point for a 400 V resonant link.

F9blocking

PCB coils cannot reach the efficiency the thermal path demands

Coil-to-coil efficiency in an inductive link is governed by the product k·Q — not by resistance alone. With no pumped coolant and no forced air, the heat a pad can shed is what sets the floor under that product.

This is the finding that decides the architecture. 191 W leaving a flat pad through ferrite into a bolted plate, with no coolant, is not a thermal design — it is a thermal impossibility. 45 W is an ordinary one. Even scaling the etched spiral to the right inductance only reaches 87%.

The decision is deliberately left open. MEC-001 is written against k·Q ≥ 49 and 120 W, not against a coil technology, because the whole argument rests on a quality factor nobody in the programme has yet measured — which is why coil bench characterisation is the first chunk on the critical path.

F1blocking

The TX power stage is specified 40× below the target

The white paper states the LMG2610 is “rated for a 600V bus and drain current of 6.4A”. The datasheet (SNOSDE2A) says otherwise on every count.

RDS(on)—170 mΩ LS / 248 mΩ HS, asymmetric by design
Package—9×7 mm QFN, RθJA 25.3 °C/W

A 3 kW tank carries 8.33 A RMS and 11.8 A peak — 2.9× the high-side absolute maximum. Conduction loss alone, ignoring all switching loss, is 14.5 W per package; at 25.3 °C/W that is a 367 °C rise against a 150 °C junction limit. The asymmetric on-resistances are optimised for flyback duty and cannot be balanced in a 50%-duty bridge.

The replacement class is a 650 V GaN FET near 30 mΩ with an integrated driver — TI’s own evaluation board for that part runs 3.6 kW. Conduction drops from 14.5 W to 2.1 W per leg.

Eight further findings, not blocking

  • Six part numbers do not name orderable parts. TPSM540 and TPSM560 are both used and neither exists; UCC27741 is UCC27714 and is rated 600 V, not the 650 V claimed.
  • The aux rails cannot work as drawn. Every real part in the TPSM family is rated 60 V maximum input; the white paper runs them from the 400 V bus. A 700 V-class offline stage is required.
  • The RX bridge is not Schottky. MUR is ultrafast silicon, and silicon Schottky does not exist at 600 V — so v1 has both a 1.7 V drop and reverse-recovery charge, the thing it set out to avoid.
  • The resonant capacitor is unspecified. ≈22 nF standing off 720 V RMS at 8.33 A — likely the largest passive in the system, and absent from the document entirely.
  • The stated coil Q is inconsistent with the stated frequency. Q = 12 at 12 µH / 0.8 Ω implies 127 kHz, not the 85 kHz nominal. One of the three numbers is wrong.
  • The firmware filter is a median, not the RMS the paper describes — so the hill-climb maximises a quantity that is not proportional to power.
  • The smoothing filter is dead code and the re-sweep guard can never fire (unsigned compare against a zero threshold).
  • The HVLV phase shift is never applied, and every control constant in that loop is a FILL THIS OUT LATER placeholder. Dead time is set nowhere; timer break inputs are disabled everywhere.

The 8 kWh discrepancy reconciles

The pack “may increase in size, not voltage”. An 8 kWh pack at 48 V is 167 Ah, and 3 kW into that is a 0.38C charge over 2.7 h — exactly the white paper’s claim. So 3 kW is sized for the future pack, and the paper describes the target vehicle rather than the present one. That is a legitimate position, recorded as vision rationale — but it means the charger must deliver well below its maximum on command, and today’s pack will never see full power.

The link is slowest when it is needed first

Handshake happens before power flows, which is the one condition where the tank sits at full unloaded Q. This is a direct consequence of F9: the efficiency requirement demands a high Q, and a high Q is a narrow filter. Two resolutions are in the requirements — accept 200 bit/s for the handshake, and switch a 22 Ω damping network that buys 22.5 kHz for 18 W at a 20 V drive.

It falls out as a safety interlock

Talking at 20 V instead of 360 V means the dock puts tens of watts into whatever is in front of it. So SYS-013 withholds power above handshake level until a receiver identifies itself — and foreign-object and absent-receiver protection come free from a mechanism that had to exist anyway.

The tracker and the link will fight. Load-shift keying modulates the reflected impedance — the exact quantity the resonance hill-climb follows. An unblanked tracker chases the data stream instead of resonance, confidently and wrongly. Recorded as FW-009 before it can be discovered on a bench.

M1 · critical path

Coil bench characterisation

Measure L, R, Q and k on a PCB coil and a Litz coil, on one fixture, at one frequency, at the real gap. The entire architecture argument rests on a Q that has never been measured, and the three v1 coil numbers are mutually inconsistent.

E1

Power architecture and part selection

Replace the TX power stage, resolve the six unorderable part numbers, design the 700 V-class aux stage, and decide whether bidirectional operation is required at all — nothing in the brief asks the Mako to export power.

Questions that need you

  • Cell charge acceptance for the present pack. 3 kW is 1.89C. If the cells take 1C, the usable rate today is 1.58 kW and the charger is deliberately oversized for the future pack.
  • Dock and vehicle ambient temperature. Without it, “conduction to chassis” has no value in °C/W and MEC-004 cannot be closed.
  • Is bidirectional operation required? The DAB is bidirectional and nothing asks for it. If not, the HV side simplifies considerably.
  • Does the Leviathan supply 400 V directly, or through a second step-up DAB? The white paper implies both readings.
  • Should the link authenticate? A dock that delivers 3 kW to anything that answers correctly is worth a deliberate decision rather than a default.
